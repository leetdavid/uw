## Open *a terminal*

Choose **File → New Terminal**, or click **▾** at the foot of the sidebar and choose **New Terminal**. You can also press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>{"`"}</kbd>.

A tab opens with your login shell in your home folder. It's named after the shell's folder, or the title the shell sets.

To run one command in a new terminal tab, type it in the [command bar](./command-bar.mdx) and press <kbd>Option</kbd> <kbd>Return</kbd>.

## Keys, paste, *and drop*

- <kbd>Cmd</kbd> shortcuts go to uw, not the shell. <kbd>Cmd</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd> and <kbd>Cmd</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> copy and paste.
- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>{"`"}</kbd> go to uw too. Other <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> keys go to the shell.
- <kbd>Option</kbd> works as Alt.
- uw's [keys for tabs and tasks](./tabs-under-tabs.mdx) work while a terminal tab has focus.
- Paste or drop a file, and its path is typed, quoted for the shell.
- Paste an image, and uw saves it to a temporary file and types that file's path. Tools such as Claude Code take an image this way.

## In *your tasks*

A terminal tab sits in tasks and nests like any other tab. [Automatic organization](./automatic-organization.mdx) files it by its title and folder, never by what the shell printed.

<Callout type="warning">
  Closing a terminal tab ends its shell and anything running in it.
</Callout>
